The iron teeth of the Empire, the heart rots but still beats.

It towers over the rest of the world like a giant over a village.
Stone walls, blackened by time and smoke, encircle the city as if it were still at war with the shadow of an ancient enemy. Beyond them are alchemists' laboratories, temples to forgotten gods, palaces with gilded roofs, and filthy quarters where rats and men live by the same laws.

The streets are like arteries: wide, noisy, forever clogged with carts, traders, news and lies.
Guards in scales that creak in the damp stand at the crossroads. They watch, but rarely intervene.

Above it all is the Citadel of the Ruling Council: a crenellated tower of stone that is found nowhere else in the world. They say it does not warm in the sun or get wet in the rain. They say that beneath it are catacombs older than the capital itself.

In the alleys are sectarians. In the bazaars are spies. In the taverns there are too many rumors, and none of them can be trusted.

The capital is not a place for the weak. But it loves those who know how to survive, fight, and listen.
Especially listen. Because the walls here remember more than people.
